This round trip exports blocks to the plain raw-file format, re-ingests them through the adapter, and reduces them again: identical results show that the real-data path is wired.

  • rtops.ingest.ingest_solar(arc, cfg, npz, meta, obid) — arrays unix, counts, hot, cold and the nominal az_deg, el_deg, el_offset_set_deg.
  • rtops.ingest.ingest_hi(arc, cfg, npz, meta, obid) — arrays freq_hz, unix, spec, hot (+ is_on for ON/OFF data), and mode, target, az/el or the OFF position.

Assumptions and limits

  • The telescope is a digital twin: signals are computed from where the virtual beam really points (command + hidden pointing errors + wind). Nothing here is an observation.
  • Hidden truth (pointing terms, wind, satellite brightness, M31 flux × effective area) is used only by the simulator and for the 'true error' columns; the control system and the reduction never read it.
  • The simulator and the pointing model use the same functional form, so recovery of the terms is optimistic. Real structures add flexure and other terms.
  • Satellite brightness and the GEO beacon level are assumptions. The catalogue error (2 km) is typical of fresh elements.
  • Beams are Gaussian (70λ/D); the Sun is a Gaussian-equivalent disc; refraction uses a standard formula in both the simulator and the controller.
  • L-band pointing is insensitive to a 0.4° error; Ku-band is not. Results scale with telescope.dish_m in the configuration.
